The problem

A raw key has no cap and no off-switch.

Companies now run fleets of agents that pay for things on their own. The moment you hand an agent a key, you've handed it your treasury with no ceiling and no brakes.

01

No ceiling

A raw signing key can spend everything. There's no per-payment cap, no daily budget, no active-hours window baked in.

02

No off-switch

When an agent misbehaves or a key leaks, there's no single lever to stop it. It keeps paying while you scramble.

03

Cutting it is manual

Killing a leaked agent today means touching every service by hand, one integration at a time — slow, error-prone, and never truly everywhere.

The honest model

What LEASH is — and isn't.

Control is real. Independence is real. Trustlessness is not — and we won't pretend otherwise.

Read the honest model ▾
  • ›It's a corporate-card model, not trustless. The chain stores the policy and records the revocation; the facilitator LEASH runs enforces the limits you set.
  • ›Identity is on-chain-resolved, not verified. We resolve the agent's external identity from chain — we don't attest to who's behind it.
  • ›Rolling daily/weekly caps are a soft budget — a guardrail the facilitator applies, not an exact on-chain guarantee.
  • ›Privy is the independent funding rail. It denies an over-fund on its own — it does not co-sign payments.
  • ›The account is co-owned: the agent holds its own key, LEASH holds only its co-signer key plus the agent's public key. Neither can spend alone.
  • ›LEASH binds agents, it doesn't mint them. You bring an agent you already run; we put it on a leash.
